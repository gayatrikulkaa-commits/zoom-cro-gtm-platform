Open `Zoom CRO Pitch (6 slides) v2.pptx` and the platform side by side. Order: slides 1 to 4, slide 5, the live platform, then slide 6 to close.

## 1. What Databook is testing, and how to position yourself

You are playing a deployment manager who is also selling. Anton (AI strategy and deployment) and Hriday are asking four things at once.

| They are testing | What they listen for | Your proof in this session |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Do you understand the product?** | Customer-back, not feature-first. The customer context graph and its five dimensions (strategic priorities, financial drivers, management intent, competitive dynamics, timing). Guided selling over "ask a chatbot". AI Studio, the Control Center, governance, closed-loop analytics. | Every Hub number says how Databook sees it and from which systems. The account page is a context graph with five angles, each labelled verified, inferred or needs validation. The Coach studio is AI Studio on one account. |
| **Can you deploy it?** | Discover workflows, map systems, normalise context, configure plays, pilot, evaluate, scale. Entity resolution. Adoption and behavior change. Data and privacy limits. | 39% to 99% resolution (Databook's figure). Connections: where insights go, where signals come from, the Zoom-recordings idea with its privacy conditions. Uplift and adoption: the slider, the behaviors, the four-step rollout. |
| **Can you sell it to Zoom?** | Their words, their numbers, a quantified and honest case, objection handling. | Slides 1 to 3 in Zoom's terms. "Beyond target". The Northwind chain. One pricing line that follows the CFO's target. |
| **Do you have executive presence?** | Answer first. Calm pace. Owning caveats before being challenged. Redirecting an interruption without losing the thread. | Short sentences, numbers you can say from memory, the return lines in section 5. |

**Your positioning, in one sentence:** "I am the person who makes Databook work inside Zoom: I turn Zoom's AI-first strategy into account-level behavior, get it live in weeks, and prove it against the CFO's target."

**Keep Zoom's objectives in view.** Name them early and tie each demo moment back to one:
- Be seen as an AI-first work platform, not a meeting tool: AI Companion, Zoom CX and Contact Center. Product perception, measured by attach and by executives on calls.
- Enterprise is about 60% of FY26 revenue and net dollar expansion is 98%: expansion and renewals matter as much as new logos.
- Win the platform conversation against Microsoft, Google, Cisco, RingCentral and Salesforce: win rate against named rivals.
- Hit and beat the FY27 guidance of $5.065B to $5.075B: beyond target, not to target.

Say the real figures only as "Zoom reports". Everything else on the platform is mock data, and you say so first.

## 3. Deck, slide by slide

**Slide 1, Zoom's need (1:15).** "Zoom has moved from video meetings to an AI-first work platform. AI Companion active users are up more than three times, and paid AI was in each of the top ten Zoom CX deals last quarter. But a platform only sells if every seller carries that story into every account. That is the gap I am here to close."

**Slide 2, the gap (1:00).** Rivals already sell the platform story. "Zoom's FY26 revenue is about 4% of the market figure Zoom cited. That is a scale comparison, not market share, and the definitions differ. The point is that the room is large and it is won account by account." Volunteer the caveat before they ask.

**Slide 3, how Databook helps (1:30).** Three outcomes: revenue growth, product perception, win rate against rivals. "The aim is not to hit the target. It is to go beyond it." Read the chain at the bottom: stated Q3 cost mandate, Contact Center, reframed pitch, dated buyer step. "You will see that chain live."

**Slide 4, the system (1:00, compress).** Map, routing engine, guidance and analytics. "Four feeds to one company: 39% by text matching, 99% with Databook, Databook's figures. Other tools tell you what happened or find more data. Databook decides what to do on this account, and proves it." Pricing: a platform fee plus about 3% of revenue above target, against a baseline the CFO agrees first. Do not linger.

**Slide 5, the loop (0:45).** "See, find, decide, guide, prove, on one account, Northwind. Let me show you."

## 7. Caveats to volunteer (first, not when challenged)

All account data is synthetic and labelled Mock data. Only Zoom's reported revenue and guidance are real. Outside data is illustrative. Dollar impacts are estimates from stated assumptions, not results. The pilot comparison is observational, with 25 and 31 deals. The Studio and the Composer are simulations. Nothing is connected to a real system.

## 8. Presence

Answer first, evidence second. Slow down on numbers. Pause after "beyond target" and after "I will not claim what I cannot source." Never apologise for the prototype; label it and move on. When you do not know, say what you would check and with whom. Look at the person who interrupted, answer, then say the return line.
